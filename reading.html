Reading Journal · Books · Ideas

A timeline of books and ideas that stayed with me.

This page is a selective reading journal rather than a complete list. I use it to keep track of books that shaped how I think, what they clarified, and the questions they left open.

Less review, more record of thought.

How I use this page

Each entry includes a short reflection and a few ideas that stayed with me. The goal is not to summarise everything, but to capture what felt most useful, difficult, or worth returning to.

April 2026

Group Processes: Dynamics Within and Between Groups

Rupert Brown & Sam Pehrson

A strong framework for understanding polarization as a group process rather than just an individual-level disagreement.

  • Conflict tends to sharpen group boundaries.
  • Identity often structures political positioning.
  • Social change depends on both dissent and collective dynamics.

Twenty-Five Years of Research Using Implicit Measures

Bertram Gawronski, Jan De Houwer & Jeffrey Sherman

Made me more careful about what implicit measures can actually tell us, and where interpretation becomes too ambitious.

  • Measures are not the same as the constructs they target.
  • Methodological excitement can exceed theoretical clarity.
  • Behavioral relevance is often conditional rather than direct.
March 2026

The Anthropology of Religion

James Bielo

Helped shift my perspective from religion as belief alone to religion as practice, embodiment, and lived experience.

  • Definitions of religion are historically shaped, not neutral.
  • Practice can matter more than formal doctrine.
  • Anthropology is useful precisely because it pays attention to lived reality.

The Psychology of Influence

Van der Pligt & Vliek

Useful for thinking about how persuasion works through emotion and identity, not only through explicit argument.

  • Emotion is part of judgment, not a distortion of it.
  • Influence often works affectively before it works rationally.
  • Attitudes become stronger when they connect to identity.
February 2026

Foreign Policy and Cleavage Theory

Research Notes

A return to the question that most interests me: how domestic divisions are translated into foreign policy preferences and party positioning.

  • Foreign policy is often less external than it first appears.
  • Domestic cleavages can travel upward into national strategy.
  • Party competition shapes what becomes “international”.

Notes on Internet Governance

Policy Reading

Reading in this area keeps reinforcing how technical governance is never purely technical, and how infrastructure always has a political dimension.

  • Infrastructure governance is also institutional governance.
  • Regulation reflects power as much as coordination.
  • Digital governance sits between domestic and international politics.
